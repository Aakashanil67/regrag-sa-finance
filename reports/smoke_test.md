## 1. What is the expected credit loss model under IFRS 9?

**Answer:**

According to the context, the expected credit loss model under IFRS 9 is a forward-looking approach that applies to all financial instruments subject to impairment accounting. Here are its key characteristics:

**Core Definition and Purpose:**
Expected credit losses are an estimate of credit losses over the life of the financial instrument [ifrs9_project_summary_2014, p.19]. The model results in more timely recognition of loan losses and is a single model applicable to all financial instruments subject to impairment accounting [ifrs9_project_summary_2014, p.2-3].

**Key Measurement Considerations:**
When measuring expected credit losses, an entity should consider: (a) the probability-weighted outcome, which reflects both the possibility that a credit loss occurs and the possibility that no credit loss occurs, neither representing a best or worst-case scenario; (b) the time value of money, with expected credit losses discounted to the reporting date; and (c) reasonable and supportable information that is available without undue cost or effort, including information about past events, current conditions, and forecasts of future conditions [ifrs9_project_summary_2014, p.19].

**Information Used:**
The model does not prescribe particular measurement methods and allows entities to use various internal and external sources of data. As the forecast horizon increases, the specificity of information used is expected to decrease. Although the model is forward-looking, historical information is considered an important anchor, but should be adjusted based on current observable data to reflect current conditions and forecast conditions [ifrs9_project_summary_2014, p.19].

**Scope:**
The model applies to financial assets classified as amortised cost and fair value through other comprehensive income, lease receivables, trade receivables, and commitments to lend money and financial guarantee contracts [ifrs9_project_summary_2014, p.14-15].

## 5. What is the National Credit Act's stated purpose?

**Refused:** False | **Latency:** 4197ms | **Cost:** $0.0029 | **Model:** claude-haiku-4-5

**Answer:**

According to the context provided, the National Credit Act, 2005 (Act No. 34 of 2005) has multiple stated purposes:

The Act was introduced to: promote a fair and non-discriminatory marketplace for access to consumer credit and provide for general regulation of consumer credit and improved standards of consumer information; promote black economic empowerment and ownership within the consumer credit industry; prohibit certain unfair credit and credit-marketing practices; promote responsible credit granting and use and prohibit reckless credit granting; provide for debt reorganisation in cases of over-indebtedness; regulate credit information; provide for registration of credit bureaus, credit providers and debt-counselling services; establish national norms and standards relating to consumer credit; promote a consistent enforcement framework relating to consumer credit; and establish the National Credit Regulator and the National Consumer Tribunal [nca_notebook_brochure, p.1-3].

More broadly, the purposes of the National Credit Act are to promote and advance the social and economic welfare of South Africans; promote a fair, transparent, competitive, sustainable, responsible, efficient, effective and accessible credit market and industry; and protect consumers through various mechanisms including promoting accessible credit markets, ensuring consistent treatment of credit products and providers, promoting responsibility in the credit market, promoting equity, and addressing imbalances in negotiating power between consumers and credit providers [nca_notebook_brochure, p.1-3].

**Refused:** False | **Latency:** 2596ms | **Cost:** $0.0027 | **Model:** claude-haiku-4-5

**Answer:**

The 2014 Retail Distribution Review proposed that greater clarity on the activities that make up advice, intermediation and outsourced services respectively, as well as on whose behalf the services are rendered, creates the foundation for a clearer set of principles and rules for intermediary remuneration [fsca_rdr_2014, p.5].
